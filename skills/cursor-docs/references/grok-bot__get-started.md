# Get started with Grok Bot

This guide takes you from install to your first finished task. You'll set up the desktop app, create a Bot with a clear job, and review its work. For a shorter help-center checklist, follow [Onboarding](https://cursor.com/help/grok-bot/onboarding.md).

Grok Bot access is included with every paid individual Cursor plan and with
the Cursor Teams plan, through an individual SuperGrok link, or with a seat
on a self-serve Grok Business plan. See [Plans and
billing](https://cursor.com/help/grok-bot/plans.md). Grok Bot needs cloud data storage, so
accounts on Privacy Mode (Legacy) are prompted to switch to Privacy Mode
before starting.

### Install the desktop app

Get the installer from the Grok Bot page of the [Cursor
dashboard](https://cursor.com/dashboard/bot): the **Download Grok Bot** setup
row installs the desktop app or copies a link you can share. If you don't
have dashboard access, ask your admin for the download link. The app runs
on macOS (Apple silicon and Intel), Windows (x64 and Arm64), and Linux
(x64 and Arm64) and checks for updates automatically. On a phone,
install Grok Bot from the App Store or Google Play; see
[Use Grok Bot on your phone](https://cursor.com/docs/grok-bot/get-started.md#use-grok-bot-on-your-phone).

### Sign in with your Cursor account

Choose **Sign in** on the welcome screen and finish authentication in
your browser. Grok Bot uses your Cursor account, and there is no separate
Grok Bot login. If your organization uses single sign-on, sign in through
your normal company flow; your existing Cursor SSO configuration applies.
For sign-in problems, see [Sign in to Grok Bot](https://cursor.com/help/grok-bot/sign-in.md).

### Meet your first Bot

After you sign in, click through a short intro. Grok Bot then sets up
your first Bot, named **Grok Bot**, and opens a chat with it.

To add more Bots, choose the **+** button in the sidebar, then **Create a
Bot**, name the Bot, and choose **Create**. On a Cursor team, share a Bot
to let teammates chat with it, and choose **Browse shared Bots** under
**+** to find Bots shared with you; see [Shared Bots](https://cursor.com/help/grok-bot/shared-bots.md).
Give each Bot one primary job in its first message and ask it to remember
how it should work. To rename it, click its name at the top of the chat to
open its details. A focused Bot builds more useful context than one
catch-all helper. For example:

> Your job is product performance. Investigate product-performance
> questions with our observability tools. Preserve links and screenshots,
> separate evidence from hypotheses, and lead with the highest-impact
> issue. Never change production settings. Remember this for future work.

### Give it a first task

A strong request states the outcome, the sources that matter, the
constraints, the deliverable, and where the Bot should stop for you. For
a five-minute first result, attach a document and try:

> Summarize this document in five bullets. List every date, decision, and
> open question in a separate section, and cite the section for each item.
> Don't change the source file.

### Sign in to the tools it needs

When the Bot reaches an app that requires authentication, it uses a login
saved in [Vault](https://cursor.com/help/grok-bot/secrets.md#what-is-vault) or a connected
password manager, or asks you for it with a Secure Form. The Bot never
sees the password, and the signed-in session persists on your computer
for future tasks. For single sign-on, a passkey, or another step a form
can't handle, it hands you the computer: click **Open computer**, finish
that step, and return control. For supported services, you can also
[connect an app](https://cursor.com/docs/grok-bot/work.md#connect-apps) instead.

### Review the result and make it stick

Ask for revisions, and name lasting preferences explicitly, like a report
format you want reused. When the process is stable, save it as a
[skill or routine](https://cursor.com/docs/grok-bot/work.md#skills-and-routines).

Before you let a Bot change external systems, read [Approvals and Auto Review](https://cursor.com/docs/grok-bot/security.md#approvals-and-auto-review). It covers approval prompts, the review model behind them, and how to keep credentials out of chat.

## Use Grok Bot on your phone

The iPhone app (iOS 18 or later) and the Android app (Android 9 or later) connect to the same Bots, conversations, routines, and cloud computer as desktop. Install Grok Bot from the App Store or Google Play and sign in with the same Cursor account; access from your plan or SuperGrok link carries over without a second purchase. From your phone you can message Bots, watch and take over the computer, approve actions, and pause or resume routines.

Some flows stay on desktop: teaching a workflow by demonstration, testing routines, and updating the computer. See [Grok Bot on mobile](https://cursor.com/help/grok-bot/mobile.md) for setup details and [Subscribe from the mobile app](https://cursor.com/help/grok-bot/mobile-purchase.md) if your account doesn't include access.

## If something doesn't work

Most setup problems clear after you fully quit and reopen the app. For symptom-by-symptom help with computers, routines, connected apps, and startup, see [Grok Bot How Tos](https://cursor.com/help/grok-bot/how-to.md#how-do-i-troubleshoot-grok-bot-computer-routine-connector-and-startup-problems) in the help center, or [contact support](https://cursor.com/help/grok-bot/get-help.md).
